# Signals workshop: from a finding to a merged fix

You'll take one security finding through the whole governed loop on the
hosted product:

1. A scanner reports a finding.
2. You triage it.
3. The crew proposes a fix.
4. The constitution decides.
5. **You approve by merging the pull request.**
6. ArgoCD ships it.

This is the loop TruStacks sells: *agents propose, policy decides, humans
approve.* About 15 minutes. Each step below was run on app.trustacks.com
when this page was written; the outcomes described are what happened.

<Callout type="info">
  **You need:** a workspace with a runner connected (0.2.15 or later), an
  LLM key configured, and an Application with a service repo and a GitHub
  platform repo connected through the TruStacks GitHub App. The
  [TruStacks 101](/getting-started/trustacks-101) walkthrough sets all of
  that up.
</Callout>

## 1. Bring in a finding

TruStacks doesn't scan. Findings come from your tools: open Dependabot,
code-scanning and secret-scanning alerts reach Signals through your runner's
read-only GitHub source, and any scanner's output can be uploaded as SARIF.

For this workshop, upload a sample:

1. Download [`dockerfile-root.sarif`](/samples/dockerfile-root.sarif). It's a
   hadolint `DL3002` result: *the container runs as root*.
2. Open it in an editor and replace `YOUR-SERVICE-REPO` with your service
   repo's name (for example `orders-api`). That's how TruStacks ties a finding
   to a connected service.
3. In **Signals**, on the **SARIF upload** row, click **Upload** and choose the
   file. You'll see **Uploaded. 1 finding created.**

The finding appears under **Incoming findings** with a **HIGH** chip. Select
it. The governed-loop stepper sits on **Finding detected**, and the
**Proposed fix** card says *No fix yet. Triage this finding, then use Propose a fix…*

If the card says the repo **isn't connected to an Application**, the name in
the SARIF file doesn't match a connected service. Fix the name and upload
again, or use **Connect this repo →**.

## 2. Triage it

Click **Triage**. This is your first human decision: *this finding is real and
worth acting on.* The stepper moves on, the card says *Ready for a fix*, and
**Propose a fix** appears. Not real? **Dismiss** it; it stays in the history.

## 3. Let the crew propose a fix

Click **Propose a fix**. The DevOps Engineer reads your service, your platform
repo and your EnvironmentProfile, and drafts the change. That takes a minute or
two. The card shows *The DevOps Engineer is drafting the fix…* and the stepper
advances on its own.

In our run, the crew didn't touch the service's Dockerfile, because TruStacks
writes only to your platform repo. It fixed the finding where it owns the
artifact: the Helm Deployment sets `securityContext.runAsNonRoot: true` and
`runAsUser: 1001`. It also noticed the Dockerfile already declared
`USER 1001`, and said so.

## 4. Read the constitution's verdict

Before any pull request opens, the signed constitution judges the change.
The card shows the result:

- **Constitution: allowed. Every rule passed.** A pull request opens.
- **Constitution: denied.** No pull request opens. The card lists each rule
  that refused the change, linked to the rule, with its message. Fix the
  cause, or the rule, and propose again.

Then look at what was proposed:

- **The change** lists every file. Ours had six: the CI workflow, a Helm chart
  (`Chart.yaml`, `values.yaml`, Deployment and Service templates) and the
  ArgoCD Application. Click one to read it in full.
- **How the crew got here** is the run log, every step the agents took
  (46 in our run).
- **The DevOps Engineer's explanation** says why, including which
  constitution rules the change satisfies.

## 5. Approve it by merging

Click **Review & merge the PR**. It opens the real pull request on your
platform repo. Review it like any other change. Then:

- **Merge it** to approve. Within about a minute TruStacks sees the merge and
  moves the finding to **shipping**.
- **Close it** if you don't want it. The finding stays where it is, and the PR
  card leaves your [Overview](/guide/overview) queue.

There is no approve button in TruStacks, on purpose. Approving is merging, in
the tool where your team already reviews code. TruStacks never merges for you.

## 6. Watch it ship

ArgoCD syncs the merged change. If your runner's ArgoCD observer is on, the
finding moves to **validated** once the app reports synced and healthy. The
stepper's last step lights up and the transition history shows every move,
with who made it.

## What just happened

- **The finding came from a tool you already run.** TruStacks isn't a scanner.
- **The crew proposed a fix it was allowed to write.** It wrote only to your
  platform repo, and it explained itself.
- **Policy decided before anyone saw a pull request.** A denied change never
  opens one.
- **You approved by merging.** Every step is recorded, and the
  [Overview](/guide/overview) reflects it: an untriaged finding is amber, a
  ready PR is green, a denied fix is red.

## Clean up

- Close the pull request if you don't want to keep the change.
- **Dismiss** the sample finding in Signals.

## Known limits

- The pull request doesn't yet say which finding it resolves
  ([#685](https://github.com/TruStacks/trustacks-mvp/issues/685)).
- **validated** needs the runner's ArgoCD observer (`observer.enabled=true`).
  Without it, the finding stays at **shipping** after the merge.
