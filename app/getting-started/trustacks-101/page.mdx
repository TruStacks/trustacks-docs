# TruStacks 101

Most teams' delivery setup grew one repo at a time. Pipelines differ between
services, nobody can say for certain which ones scan images or pin their
actions, and fixing a gap means someone hand-writing the same workflow again.
TruStacks puts an agent crew on that work: the agents read your repos and
propose platform changes as pull requests, a signed policy decides what is
allowed, a human always approves, and your own ArgoCD ships it. We author the
artifact; you run it on your stack.

**Agents propose. Policy decides. Humans approve.**

By the end of this tour you will have read your workspace at a glance, run an
analysis, followed one change from proposal to a pull request waiting for
your review, then run a gap analysis and seen your maturity score and what it
would take to raise it.

<Callout type="info">
  This page assumes a workspace that is already set up: a runner connected,
  an LLM key saved, and one Application with a service repo and a platform
  repo. If you are starting from nothing, do the
  [Quickstart](/getting-started/hosted-quickstart) first and come back.
</Callout>

## Stop 1 · Overview

**What you do.** Sign in at app.trustacks.com. You land on **Overview**.

<figure className="ts-shot">
  <img src="/guide/overview.png" alt="" />
  <figcaption>Stop 1 · Overview for BetaOrg1: the bronze maturity badge and bar top right, the "Needs your attention" queue, and the "Policy in force" card below it.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Why it matters.** The home screen is about decisions, not vanity counts.
**Needs your attention** lists what is stuck, failed or waiting on a human,
most urgent first, and links to where you fix it. **Policy in force** shows
which rules actually refused a proposal recently. If nothing is waiting, it
says **Nothing is waiting on you.** rather than inventing a health score.

The colours tell you where to start. **Red** means something is broken or
refused: no runner checking in, a failed run, a proposal the policy denied, or
an unhealthy app. **Amber** means a decision is waiting on you, such as a
finding to triage or a fix to approve. **Green** means work is done and ready
for you to review: a PR, or an analysis to promote. Cards clear themselves when
you deal with the thing in the world. To get rid of a PR card you don't want,
close the PR; you don't have to merge it. That works for PRs on a platform repo
connected through the TruStacks GitHub App, for a service that still belongs to
an Application.

More: [Overview](/guide/overview), including what clears each card.

## Stop 2 · Stack

**What you do.** Open **Stack** in the sidebar.

<figure className="ts-shot">
  <img src="/guide/stack.png" alt="" />
  <figcaption>Stop 2 · Stack: the "Declared · Observed · Verified" header with the connected github source slot, and the Security Plane category cards with their Observed and Verified lines.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Why it matters.** You declare the tools you run in one file,
`environment-profile.yaml`, in your platform repo. Every agent reads it
before proposing anything, and only declared tools get pipeline steps. Stack
shows each category three ways, never blended: what you **declared**, what a
read-only source in your cluster **observed**, and what your latest gap
analysis **verified**. A declaration is a claim; an observation is evidence.
The gap between them is what an auditor would find, so you see it first.

More: [Stack](/guide/stack) · [Environment Profile](/reference/environment-profile).

## Stop 3 · Applications and services

**What you do.** Open **Applications**, then **Manage** on your Application.

<figure className="ts-shot">
  <img src="/guide/application-detail.png" alt="" />
  <figcaption>Stop 3 · Application detail for betaorg1-dogfood: the Renderer, GitOps root and Target clusters cards, betaorg1-dogfood-platform as the Platform / GitOps repo, and betaorg1-dogfood-service and trustacks-java-dogfood under Member services.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Why it matters.** An **Application** is the unit TruStacks works in: one
or more service repos, the one platform repo their delivery config lands in,
and the target clusters it deploys to, each with a tier (`dev`, `uat` or
`prod`). The agents read service repos and never write generated files into
your application code. Every change lands as a pull request in the platform
repo, where your reviewers already look.

More: [Applications, services and proposals](/guide/applications) · [Applications, repositories and services](/concepts/applications-repositories-services).

## Stop 4 · Run an analysis on a service

**What you do.** Click a service (for example `betaorg1-dogfood-service`).
Under **Run a new analysis**, leave the ref at `main` and click **Run
analysis**.

<figure className="ts-shot">
  <img src="/screenshots/101/04-analysis.png" alt="" />
  <figcaption>Stop 4 · A finished analysis of betaorg1-dogfood-service: the Activity log with the Coordinator and Code Reviewer turns and the Code Reviewer's findings.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Why it matters.** Your runner clones the repo inside your cluster and the
**Code Reviewer** describes it: framework, language, runtime, entrypoints,
and whether tests, a Dockerfile and CI exist. A deterministic detector runs
first, so a known framework is named as a fact, not a guess. An analysis
changes nothing; it is the grounding for everything the crew proposes next.
Each run's detail page shows the crew's turns as they happen.

More: [Service detail](/guide/applications#service-detail).

## Stop 5 · From proposal to pull request

**What you do.** On the same service, open the **Analyses** tab, click
**promote →** on the finished analysis, then **Promote to proposal**.

<figure className="ts-shot">
  <img src="/guide/proposal-detail.png" alt="" />
  <figcaption>Stop 5 · Proposal detail for betaorg1-dogfood-service: the Constitution's clean verdict (no violations), the advisory gap row, and the green "Pull request opened" banner.</figcaption>
</figure>

{/* asset: public/screenshots/101/05-github-pr.png */}
<figure className="ts-shot ts-shot--pending">
  <figcaption>Stop 5 · The resulting pull request on GitHub in the platform repo: Summary, Blast radius, Rollback, Rule citations and the file list, open and awaiting review.</figcaption>
  <span className="ts-shot__file">Save as <code>public/screenshots/101/05-github-pr.png</code></span>
</figure>

**Why it matters.** The **DevOps Engineer** reads the analysis, your Stack
and your platform repo, and writes a reusable CI workflow, a Helm chart and
an ArgoCD Application. Before anything reaches GitHub, the files are
validated and judged by the signed **constitution**. A deny stops the run
and cites each rule; no pull request opens. On allow, the pull request
explains what changed, its blast radius and how to roll it back. The merge is
yours; no agent can do it. Emitted ArgoCD Applications use manual sync, so
deploying is a second human decision.

More: [A proposal, from your side](/guide/applications#a-proposal-from-your-side) · [Proposal lifecycle](/reference/proposal-lifecycle).

## Stop 6 · Gap analysis

**What you do.** Open **Gap analyses** and click **Run new gap analysis**.
Open the run when it finishes.

<figure className="ts-shot">
  <img src="/guide/gap-analysis-detail.png" alt="" />
  <figcaption>Stop 6 · A finished gap analysis for betaorg1-dogfood: the Activity log with the Constitution, Coordinator, Baseline Security and SOC2 turns, and the Maturity card with the bronze tier badge.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Why it matters.** A gap analysis answers *is this Application set up
correctly?* The **Coordinator** checks your declared stack against the rules
your runner enforces, then consults two specialists: **Baseline Security**
on scanning, secrets and supply chain, and **SOC2** on the control families
its findings map to. The score is computed by the runner before any model
writes a word, so the number you brief upward is not an LLM's opinion.

More: [Gap analyses](/guide/gap-analyses).

## Stop 7 · Maturity

**What you do.** Open **Maturity**.

<figure className="ts-shot">
  <img src="/guide/maturity.png" alt="" />
  <figcaption>Stop 7 · Maturity for betaorg1-dogfood: the bronze tier badge and score bar with its confirmed / counted / if verified legend, the "Open gaps" cards with Draft pipeline PR, and the "Path to gold" card.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Why it matters.** Your tier (bronze, silver or gold) comes from the gap
analysis you just ran, and the bar separates what TruStacks **confirmed**
from evidence from claims it could not check, which count half. **Open gaps**
names what is pulling the score down; **Draft pipeline PR** asks the crew to
add a missing tool's step. **Path to gold** says what is yours to do, what is
ours, and whether gold is reachable today. If it is not, the page says so.

More: [Maturity](/guide/maturity) · [Verified, claimed and disputed](/concepts/verified-claimed-disputed).

## Stop 8 · Rules

**What you do.** Open **Rules** and click a rule, for example
`practice.workflow_has_test_step`.

<figure className="ts-shot">
  <img src="/guide/rules.png" alt="" />
  <figcaption>Stop 8 · Rules: the gate strip, the rule list, and practice.workflow_has_test_step selected with its "Open · Apache-2.0" badge and the "Enforced outcome · Rego" source.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Why it matters.** This is the exact policy your proposals are judged
against, as your runner loaded it, with the Rego source. The constitution is
public and Apache 2.0, so your security team can read every rule. Your own
rules go in an overlay, and an overlay can only make the gate stricter: from
runner 0.2.14 the runner rejects an overlay that reaches outside its own
namespace.

More: [Rules](/guide/rules) · [Constitution](/reference/constitution).

## Stop 9 · Signals

**What you do.** Open **Signals**. Click **Connect a source** to see the
wizard; to see findings, choose **SARIF upload** and upload a scan.

<figure className="ts-shot">
  <img src="/guide/signals.png" alt="" />
  <figcaption>Stop 9 · Signals: the Sources panel with GitHub "Connected · read-only verified", the findings list, and one finding selected with Dismiss and the six-step governed loop. If the list is empty, upload a SARIF scan first.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Why it matters.** Findings come from the tools you already run, or are
observed in GitHub by your runner with a read-only token that never leaves
your cluster. TruStacks is not another scanner. **Triage** a real finding,
then **Propose a fix**: the fix is a normal proposal, with the same policy
gate and the same human review.

More: [Signals](/guide/signals).

## Stop 10 · Compliance

**What you do.** Open **Compliance**, then **Download an audit report
(PDF) →**, choose **PDF (auditor-ready)** and **Generate report**.

<figure className="ts-shot">
  <img src="/guide/compliance.png" alt="" />
  <figcaption>Stop 10 · Compliance: the SOC2 evidence card for the last 90d, with control ids, titles and event counts.</figcaption>
</figure>

<figure className="ts-shot">
  <img src="/guide/audit-reports.png" alt="" />
  <figcaption>Stop 10 · Audit Reports: control family SOC2, PDF (auditor-ready), the default 7-day window, and the "Report download started" confirmation.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Why it matters.** Every governed action leaves a record: an analysis, a
proposal and its verdict, a pull request, a gap analysis. TruStacks maps
those to the SOC2 controls they evidence and counts them. It shows evidence,
not pass or fail marks; your auditor judges. The PDF covers the window you
pick and is generated on demand.

More: [Compliance and audit reports](/guide/compliance).

## What you have now

- A view of what needs a human, and which rules actually bite.
- Your stack as declared, observed and verified, side by side.
- A delivery change written by the crew, checked by signed policy, and
  waiting on your approval as a pull request.
- A maturity tier with the verified part separated from the claimed part,
  and a named path to the next tier.
- SOC2 evidence that accrues from the work itself.
