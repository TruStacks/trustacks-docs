# Portfolio

**Where:** **Portfolio**, at the very top of the sidebar (`#/portfolio`).

Portfolio is the view for whoever answers for the workspace. It asks three
questions about the changes that reached your platform repos: how many there
were, how many policy checked, and how many a named person approved. It
names every change behind a gap, with a link to its PR, and then shows where
each Application is exposed, which runtimes are past or near their end of
life, and which standards are in force.

**Who sees it:** workspace owners. Other roles don't see the sidebar item,
and the screen says *The portfolio is for workspace owners*. Today every
member of a workspace is an owner (see [Team](/guide/team)), so everyone in
the workspace sees it.

## What you see

### Header

- **Last 30 days** (the default) or **Last 90 days**. These are the only two
  windows.
- An **Application** picker, shown when the workspace has two or more
  Applications. **All applications** sums across every platform repo;
  picking one narrows the cards and lists to that Application's platform
  repo.
- **Export PDF** downloads the view you're looking at, for the same window
  and the same Application filter. See [The PDF](#the-pdf).

### The three cards

Under **Changes to your platform repos, last N days**:

| Card | What it counts |
|---|---|
| **Changes** | Every pull request merged into the default branch of your platform repo (or repos) in the window, whoever opened it: TruStacks, a person on your team, or a bot. |
| **Checked by policy** | Changes with a TruStacks policy verdict on record (**allow** or **deny**). |
| **Approved by a named person** | Changes approved in your git host by a person who is not a bot, and not only the person who wrote or merged it. |

**Checked by policy** is only knowable for changes TruStacks opened, because
only those carry a verdict TruStacks recorded. A policy check that errored
judged nothing, so it doesn't count. When every change in the window came
from TruStacks, the card reads **N of M**. When any change was opened
outside TruStacks, the card reads **Checked: N of the M TruStacks opened**
and says how many changes were opened outside TruStacks: whether policy
checked those isn't visible, because it needs your platform repo's CI
result. On GitHub, reading that result needs the `checks: read` permission
on the TruStacks GitHub App, which it doesn't have. The card never claims
coverage across changes it can't see.

**Approved by a named person** sorts each change into one of three groups:

- **Approved**: at least one approver who is a person, and not the author
  or the person who merged it.
- **Self-approved**: a person wrote the change, and the only approvals came
  from that person or from whoever merged it. Self-approval is counted on
  its own. It is never counted as approved, and never as unapproved.
- **No approval recorded**: no approval in the git host, or approved only by
  a bot. An approval given somewhere else, in a meeting or a ticket, doesn't
  show in the git host, so the screen says *no approval recorded*, not
  *nobody approved*.

A self-approved or unapproved change that a person on your team has since
reviewed in TruStacks moves to its own line, **reviewed after merge** (see
[Record a review after merge](#record-a-review-after-merge)). It is never
counted as approved. The card then reads, for example, *0 of 2 approved by
a named person*, with *1 reviewed after merge* and *1 with no approval
recorded* listed under it.

Two rules apply to every approval:

- **Bots never count as a person.** Dependabot, Renovate, GitHub Apps
  (including TruStacks' own), GitLab project and group token users and the
  Azure DevOps build service are all treated as bots.
- **A person approving a change TruStacks opened is a named approval.** The
  agent wrote that change, so nobody is approving their own work. This holds
  even when the same person approved and merged it, and even on Azure DevOps
  and GitLab, where the git host lists the owner of the token TruStacks used
  as the change's author. TruStacks uses its own record of who wrote the
  change, not the host's author field. This is *agents propose, policy
  decides, humans approve*, counted.

With no platform repo connected, the cards are replaced by *No platform repo
is connected, so there are no changes to count*, with a link to Applications. Zero changes there means nothing
to count, not a clean record.

### The gaps behind the numbers

Under the cards, each gap is a named list. A list appears only when it has
something in it:

- **Self-approved**
- **No approval recorded**
- **Reviewed after merge**
- **Opened by TruStacks, no policy verdict on record**: the policy check
  errored or never ran for these.

Each entry shows the PR title, the repo and PR number, who merged it, when,
the reason it is in the list, and **Open the PR**.

#### Record a review after merge

Every entry under **Self-approved** and **No approval recorded** has a
**Record a review after merge** button. It is for a change that shipped
without a named person's approval, when someone on your team has since
looked at it and wants that on the record under their own name. Like the
rest of Portfolio, it is for workspace owners.

The form asks for:

- **What you reviewed, and why it's acceptable** (required): 10 to 1,000
  characters, in your own words. It starts empty.
- **Outcome** (optional): *No concerns* or *Follow-up needed*. It starts at
  *Not stated*.
- **Link** (optional): an http(s) URL, for example a ticket or a follow-up
  PR.

TruStacks records you as the reviewer, from your sign-in, and the time;
neither can be set in the form. The change then moves to **Reviewed after
merge**, which lists each review: who, when, the outcome, the reason and
the link, beside the git host's own verdict on the change.

What a review after merge is not:

- **Not an approval.** A reviewed change is never counted under **Approved
  by a named person**. The review came after the change shipped, and the
  screen says so.
- **Not a correction.** The change's own record, including the git host's
  verdict, stays as it was. Each review is stored on its own.
- **Not editable.** A review can't be edited or deleted, and each person can
  record one review of a change. A change approved by a named person can't
  be reviewed this way.

**All N changes, with who approved them** opens a table of every change in
the window: the change (a TruStacks icon marks the ones TruStacks opened),
when it merged and who merged it, the named approvers, the approval group,
and the policy column (**Checked**, **No verdict**, or **Not visible yet**
for a change opened outside TruStacks). For a change TruStacks opened, the
policy column links to its proposal.

### Exposure

One row per Application, read from its latest weekly snapshot:

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Application** | Links to the Application. |
| **Owner** | The owner team, or the owner email, set on the Application's **Owner** card. **No owner** when neither is set. |
| **Tier** | The maturity tier and coverage percent, with the trend: *flat since* a date when coverage hasn't moved for two weeks or more, or the change in points over the window (*+N pts in 30 days*). **Not scored yet** when the Application has no snapshot. |
| **Image scanning**, **Secret scanning**, **SAST / SCA** | Whether each tooling category is covered. |
| **Tests in CI**, **Pinned actions** | Whether each practice is in place. |
| **Prod gate** | Whether production promotion is manual. |
| **Runtime** | The most urgent detected runtime and its support status: *past end of life*, *security fixes end* a date, *supported* or *end of life unknown*, plus how many other services there are. **Not recorded** or **None detected** when there is nothing to show. Every service is listed under [Runtimes](#runtimes). |

The scanning, practice and prod gate cells use one legend:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✓ | verified |
| ~ | claimed, not verified |
| ✗ | missing (also *partial*, and *claimed, but not found*) |
| `—` | not applicable: the Application has no prod cluster |
| ? | not recorded |

A missing value is shown as not recorded, never as fine. Verified, claimed
and missing are never blended; see [Maturity](/guide/maturity) for how each
is decided.

#### Draft the fix

Some scanning cells carry a **Draft the fix** button. It is the same gap
remediation as **Draft pipeline PR** on the [Maturity](/guide/maturity) page.

1. **You choose the tool.** The button opens **Draft the fix:
   &lt;category&gt;**, a list of the tools TruStacks can add for that
   category, with the recommended one pre-selected. It says: *Merging this
   PR declares &lt;tool&gt; for &lt;category&gt; in your Stack file and adds
   its CI step.* **Cancel** drafts nothing.
2. **The crew opens one pull request** that adds the tool's step to the
   workflow your service actually calls and declares the tool in
   `environment-profile.yaml`. The PR names who chose the tool, by display
   name, never by email.
3. **Policy checks it, and a person on your team reviews and merges it.**
   Nothing merges on its own.

A fix is scoped: it changes nothing but that workflow, only by adding to
it, plus the one entry in your Stack file. See
[What a fix changes](/guide/maturity#what-a-fix-changes). Once the request
is sent, the cell reads **Fix in progress → view**.

The button appears on a cell only when all of these hold:

- The cell is a tooling category (**Image scanning**, **Secret scanning**,
  **SAST / SCA**) that is missing, partial, or claimed but not found.
- The Application's latest snapshot comes from a finished gap analysis of
  its own platform repo, which lists that category as an open gap and
  recommends a tool for it.
- Once your runners have reported which tools they can add, at least one
  tool for that category is on every active runner.
- At least one of the Application's services has a finished analysis.

If any of these is missing, the cell has no button, so the button never
offers a fix that would be refused.

#### A fix already on record

Once a fix has been drafted, the cell shows where the newest one stands.
This comes from TruStacks' record of the run, not from the page, so leaving
Portfolio and coming back shows the same thing:

| The cell reads | What it means | **Draft the fix** offered again |
|---|---|---|
| **Fix in progress → view** | The crew is working on it. The link opens the run. | No |
| **Fix PR open → review**, and **See the run** | The pull request is open. The first link opens the PR in your git host, the second the run. | No |
| **Fix merged** | Someone on your team merged it. The cell adds that it reads verified once TruStacks sees the step and the tool is declared in `environment-profile.yaml`. | No |
| **Fix failed → view** | The run ended in an error. | Yes |
| **Fix stalled → view** | The run was still queued or running after 15 minutes, so it is treated as lost. | Yes |
| **Fix PR closed → view** | The PR was closed without merging. | Yes |
| **Last fix opened no PR → view** | The run finished without opening a PR, for example because there was nothing to change. | Yes |

Once the category is covered, a merged or closed fix is no longer shown.

**One fix per gap at a time.** While a fix for a category is in progress,
TruStacks refuses a second one for the same gap, whether it is asked for
from Portfolio, from Maturity or from a tab that was left open. The cell
then shows the run already going. A run still in progress after 15 minutes
no longer blocks a new one.

Merging the pull request is the claim: it declares the tool you chose. The
category then reads claimed, not verified, until a gap analysis finds the
tool's pinned step in your platform repo's workflow. When the PR couldn't
declare the tool, it says so and gives the lines to add yourself. See
[After you merge](/guide/maturity#after-you-merge).

Some cells never get the button:

- **Practices** (**Tests in CI**, **Pinned actions**, **Prod gate**).
  Declaring a practice is your attestation, not something the crew writes
  for you.
- **Runtimes.** There is no governed fix flow for upgrading a runtime, and
  the DevOps Engineer doesn't upgrade runtimes.
- **Claimed cells** (~). A claim that isn't verified yet doesn't need a new
  pipeline step; what it lacks is verification.
- **A platform repo shared by two Applications.** The fix is built from the
  platform repo, so it could land on the other Application. Neither gets the
  button.

### Runtimes

One row per service, most urgent first: past end of life, then security
fixes only (soonest end first), then detected with an unknown end of life,
then not detected, then supported.

Each runtime is **detected by the Code Reviewer** from the service's repo.
It is read by the model, not verified. TruStacks checks it against the
endoflife.date data bundled with the Control Plane.

A summary above the table leads with what needs action, for example *1
service runs a runtime past end of life (dotnet 6): no more security
fixes*, or *2 services are on a runtime that gets security fixes only
(...); the first loses them in 12 days*.

The **End of life** column reads:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **past end of life** (with the date) | No more security fixes. |
| **security fixes end** a date | Still patched for security, nothing else. |
| **supported** | Inside its supported window. |
| **end of life unknown** | Detected, but not found in the end-of-life data. |
| **not detected yet** | The service has no finished analysis. |
| **not recorded yet** | The Application's snapshot doesn't record runtimes, or there is no snapshot. |

A service TruStacks couldn't detect is listed and counted, never dropped and
never shown as supported.

### Standards

**Rules in force**, as your runners last reported them:

- **TruStacks constitution**: how many rules, and whether its signature was
  verified (**signature verified**, **signature not verified**, or
  **signature status not reported**).
- **Your own rules**: **none in force**, the number in force from a signed
  bundle with its signature status, or the number read from your platform
  repo. Rules read from the platform repo are unsigned by design: your
  repo's review is the trust anchor. See
  [Add your own rule](/guide/rules#add-your-own-rule).
- If no runner has reported yet, the panel says the rules in force aren't
  known. It never says there are none.
- If your runners report different rules, the panel says so and shows the
  newest report.

**Per Application**, every practice the latest gap analysis scored, with
the same marks as Exposure. The headline reads *N of M scored applications
meet every practice, verified*. Only verified counts as met: a claimed
practice is counted on its own (*N claimed, not verified*), never as met.
An Application with no snapshot reads **Not scored yet**.

### The PDF

**Export PDF** renders the current view, for the same window and
Application filter, as a printable PDF. Like the screen, it is for
workspace owners only.

Page one is the change-control summary, headed *Change control · changes to
your platform repos*: the three cards (including the *reviewed after merge*
line) and every change behind a gap, with links to the PRs, a **Reviewed
after merge · N** section with each review's reviewer, date, outcome,
reason and link beside the git host's verdict, then the full change table. It is
written to be usable as evidence of change control, for example for SOC2
CC8.1 (change management). It is evidence for your auditor to weigh, not a
certification. Exposure, Runtimes and Standards follow.

The PDF uses the screen's wording, including what it can't see:

- When any change was opened outside TruStacks, the policy card reads
  **Checked: N of the M TruStacks opened**, never *N of all*.
- **No approval recorded**, never *nobody approved*.
- **Self-approved** is its own line, never counted as approved.
- **Reviewed after merge** is its own line too, never counted as approved.
- The bot rule is stated on the page.
- A runtime with no detection, or an unknown end of life, is printed as
  such, never as supported.

The response carries an `X-TruStacks-Content-Sha256` header with the PDF's
SHA-256, as the audit report PDF does, so a forwarded copy can be checked
against what the Control Plane produced.

## What to do here

- Open the **Self-approved** and **No approval recorded** lists first. Each
  entry links to the PR, so you can ask the person who merged it. Once
  someone has looked at it, **Record a review after merge** puts that on
  the record under their name, without counting it as approved.
- If **Checked by policy** reads *N of the M TruStacks opened*, the rest of
  your changes reached the platform repo without going through TruStacks.
  Whether your CI checked them is a question for your CI, not this screen.
- Use **Last 90 days** for a quarter's view, and the **Application** picker
  to look at one platform repo at a time.
- In **Exposure**, a ✗ or ~ points at a gap you can close; the Application's
  [gap analysis](/guide/gap-analyses) and [Maturity](/guide/maturity) say
  what closing it takes. A red runtime is past its end of life; see
  [Runtimes](#runtimes) for every service.
- On a tooling gap with **Draft the fix**, click it, choose the tool, and
  the crew opens the pull request. When the cell reads **Fix PR open →
  review**, review and merge it; merging declares the tool. If it reads **Fix failed → view**, open the run to see why
  before drafting it again.
- When an auditor asks for change-control evidence, pick the window and
  Application and click **Export PDF**.

## How it connects

- Changes TruStacks opened are [proposals](/guide/applications#proposals);
  their verdicts come from the [rules](/guide/rules) in force.
- The tier, coverage and cell statuses come from
  [Maturity](/guide/maturity), snapshotted weekly.
- The owner is set on each Application's page in
  [Applications](/guide/applications).

## Beta limits

- **Policy checks on changes opened outside TruStacks aren't counted.** On
  GitHub that needs the App's `checks: read` permission. On Azure DevOps and
  GitLab, TruStacks doesn't use pipeline status for this.
- **Approvals only come from the git host.** An approval given anywhere else
  isn't seen.
- **Exposure, Runtimes and per-Application practices are as fresh as the
  current week's snapshot.**
- **Runtimes are detected, not verified.** The Code Reviewer reads them from
  the repo.
- **The change table lists at most 2,000 changes per window.** When a window
  holds more, it shows the newest and says so.
