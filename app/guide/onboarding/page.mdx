# Get set up

**Where:** **Get set up** in the sidebar (`#/onboarding`). A green number
on the sidebar item shows how many steps remain. A new workspace is sent
here automatically on first sign-in.

{/* screenshot-placeholder: Get set up wizard, step list on the left with two steps ticked, "Enroll your runner" open on the right. Save as public/guide/onboarding.png */}

The wizard is the path from an empty workspace to your first governed pull
request. It is a checklist with shortcuts, not a gate: you can open any step
in any order, skip steps and come back.

## What you see

A numbered step list on the left and the open step on the right. Each step
has a short explanation, its own controls, and at the bottom **Mark done &
continue** (or **Finish** on the last step) and **Skip for now**.

| # | Step | What you do there |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tell us about your team** | Optional. A short form (company, role, what you want to use TruStacks for, how you heard about us). **Save & continue**. |
| 2 | **Enroll your runner** | **Generate enrollment token**, copy the `helm install` command and run it against your cluster. The step waits, showing **Waiting for your runner to connect…**, and ticks itself when the runner checks in. Same component as [Runners](/guide/runners). |
| 3 | **Connect a repo** | If you have no Application yet, create one inline (the name defaults to `my-services`). Then **Connect a repo** opens the connect wizard. See [Applications](/guide/applications#connecting-repos). |
| 4 | **Declare what you run** | Explains `environment-profile.yaml`, lists the twelve tooling categories, and shows a starter file with **Copy YAML**. **Got it** acknowledges the step. **See what you've declared on Stack** opens [Stack](/guide/stack). |
| 5 | **Set your LLM provider** | **Open LLM settings** takes you to [Settings](/guide/settings). |
| 6 | **Run your first analysis** | **Go to your services** takes you to the services list, where you open a service and press **Run analysis**. |

When everything is complete, a banner says so and you can still revisit any
step.

## What it means

- **"Done" mostly comes from the server, not from your clicks.** The runner
  step is done when a runner is actually checking in; the repo step when a
  repo is connected; the LLM step when a key is configured; the analysis step
  when one has run. **Mark done & continue** ticks a step optimistically, but
  the next status refresh shows the real state.
- **Step 4 is the exception.** Nothing on the server can tell whether you
  read the explanation, so **Got it** is stored in your browser. Committing
  the file to your platform repo is what actually matters.
- **Step 1 and step 4 are different "profiles".** Step 1 is *who you are*.
  Step 4 is *what you run*: a file in your platform repo that every agent
  reads and that your maturity score is computed from. The wizard says this
  explicitly because the names collide.

## What to do here

Work top to bottom on the first visit. The order that avoids dead ends:

1. Enroll the runner first. Nothing runs without it.
2. Create an Application before connecting repos. The connect wizard refuses
   to start without one, because every repo must belong to an Application.
3. Connect the **platform repo** (role: Platform) and at least one **service
   repo** (role: Service) to that Application.
4. Commit `environment-profile.yaml` to the root of the platform repo. Fill
   in the tools you run. Leave the `practices:` block commented until each
   line is true of your pipeline (see
   [Verified, claimed and disputed](/concepts/verified-claimed-disputed)).
5. Set the LLM key.
6. Run the first analysis, then promote it to a proposal.

The [hosted quickstart](/getting-started/hosted-quickstart) walks the same
path with commands.

## Why the starter profile has blanks

The starter `environment-profile.yaml` ships with two configuration entries
filled in (GitHub Actions as CI, ArgoCD as GitOps controller) and every
claim about your practice commented out. That is deliberate. A `true` under
`practices:` is an attestation that the maturity score counts. TruStacks
never writes one for you, because a claim about your pipeline has to be
yours.

## How it connects

- Step 2 → [Runners](/guide/runners)
- Step 3 → [Applications](/guide/applications)
- Step 4 → [Stack](/guide/stack) and the
  [environment profile reference](/reference/environment-profile)
- Step 5 → [Settings](/guide/settings)
- Step 6 → [Applications → services → analyses](/guide/applications#analyses)

## Beta limits

- Step 1 says you can fill the team profile in later from Settings. Today
  the form only exists in this wizard; revisit step 1 to change it.
- Step 4 explains the file and hands you a starter; it does not open a PR
  that writes the file for you.
- The **Connect a repo** button lands on the cross-application services
  list. Its empty state mentions `make seed` and Gitea, which only apply to
  local development. On the hosted product, use **+ Connect repo** and pick
  GitHub.
