# Verified, claimed and disputed

The maturity score is the number customers show other people: their
leadership, their auditors, their own teams. So it has to be a number
TruStacks can stand behind. That rules out the easy design, where you list
your tools in a YAML file and get a gold badge.

This page explains the design TruStacks uses instead.

## A declaration is a claim, not a score

You describe your delivery stack in `environment-profile.yaml`, in your
platform repo. Two kinds of thing go in it:

- **Tooling categories**: *do you have the tool?* For example `trivy`
  under `image_scanning`.
- **Behaviors**: *do you use it correctly?* Five keys under `practices:`,
  such as `tests_in_ci: true`.

Both are statements you make about your own pipeline. A statement is
useful: the agents read it and build what you declared. But writing
`tests_in_ci: true` does not make your CI run tests. So TruStacks treats
every declaration as a **claim**, and gives full credit only when it has
**evidence**.

## Four states

| State | What it means | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| **verified** | You declared it, and TruStacks observed it. | full |
| **claimed** | You declared it, and TruStacks has no evidence either way. | half |
| **disputed** | You declared it, and what TruStacks observed contradicts it. | none |
| **missing** | You have not declared it. | none |

Three properties follow from these weights, and each is deliberate:

- **Gold cannot be reached on claims alone.** Gold is 90%; all-claimed is
  50%.
- **Saying so still beats silence.** A claim outranks a missing row, because
  an honest declaration is information.
- **A disproved claim earns nothing.** Half credit for something the
  evidence contradicts would reward the claim TruStacks just refuted.

And one asymmetry: **missing evidence never demotes you.** If TruStacks
cannot look, the row stays claimed. Only contrary evidence makes a row
disputed.

## Where evidence comes from

TruStacks verifies what it can observe in the delivery path, not what it is
told:

- **Pipeline content.** A declared scanner counts as verified when its step
  is actually in your pipeline. The pinned actions in TruStacks' curated
  tool packs are how a step is recognized.
- **Policy verdicts.** The constitution's behavior rules inspect the
  artifacts on your latest judged proposal: does the workflow have a test
  step and a lint step, do prod ArgoCD Applications use manual sync, do
  containers run as non-root, are actions pinned to SHAs?
- **The Code Reviewer's analysis**, for example whether the service has
  tests at all.

Some rows have no evidence source yet. Those can only ever count half. The
[Maturity](/guide/maturity) screen names them and calculates the highest
score you can actually reach (the *ceiling*), so it never promises a gold
you cannot get. Closing that gap is TruStacks' job, not yours, and the
screen says so.

## Never pre-fill an attestation

TruStacks hands you files: a starter environment profile, generated
workflows, proposals. None of them will ever contain a claim about your
practice already set to `true`.

**Configuration may have opinionated defaults; claims may not.** The
starter profile fills in `github_actions` as your CI platform and `argocd`
as your GitOps controller, because those configure what the agents build.
It ships every `practices:` line commented out, because each one is an
attestation the score counts. Uncomment a line when it is true of you.

The same rule holds for the agents. When Maturity offers **Draft pipeline
PR** for a missing scanner, the crew adds the step to your pipeline but does
not declare the tool in your profile. The PR closes the pipeline half; the
declaration is yours to make, and your score moves when you make it.

Why so strict? An understated score annoys you. An overstated one misleads
whoever you show it to. On a product whose promise is *agents propose,
policy decides, humans approve*, manufacturing the human's claim is the one
thing an artifact must not do.

## Scores can fall, and that is fine

When TruStacks learns to verify a new row, or a new rule adds a row, a
score can drop with nothing in your setup changing. The app shows a drop in
amber, not red, and the change line says how much. Treat the **verified**
segment of the bar as the part you can repeat to an auditor.

## One score per Application

A score is computed from one environment profile, which belongs to one
Application (it lives in that Application's platform repo). Two
Applications are scored separately and never averaged together: an average
that hides one bronze among four golds is the same false reassurance this
whole design exists to avoid. See
[Applications, repositories and services](/concepts/applications-repositories-services).
